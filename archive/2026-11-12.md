# Daily Dev Digest — 2026-11-12

> Theme: Open weights and open apps keep pushing into places closed models used to own — meeting notes on your laptop, a trillion-parameter MIT model, Meta's Muse on a rival cloud.

## Picks

### Yogurt — a free, open-source Granola alternative for meeting notes
Meeting-notes app for Mac, Windows and Linux that records calls without a bot, then transcribes and summarizes with models running on your own machine. MIT-licensed per its listing. Launched on Hacker News on Nov 11. On the radar because local-first, bot-free note-taking is a clean test of how far on-device models have come. Only secondary listing found; check the repo before relying on it.

### LongCat-2.5 — Meituan's 1.6T multimodal MoE, reportedly MIT-licensed weights
Reported as a 1.6T-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts with about 48B active parameters per token, built for long-horizon agent tasks, weights on Hugging Face under MIT. On the radar because it is one of the largest permissively licensed open-weight releases. Caveat: the 2.5 name appears in a single source I found, and the model card reportedly carries a reservation I could not read. Verify on Meituan's Hugging Face page.

### Muse models on Google Cloud — Meta's Muse Ember, Spark 1.3 and Wonder in Model Garden
A report dated today says Meta's Muse Ember, Muse Spark 1.3 and Muse Wonder are available through Google Cloud's Model Garden, with Sundar Pichai publicly welcoming them. On the radar because Meta models landing on a rival hyperscaler's catalog shows distribution, not just weights, is where the competition is moving. Single-outlet report; details unconfirmed.
