# Daily Dev Digest — 2026-10-26

> Theme: Agents leave the terminal — they click through your apps, ship as CLI-native models, and run into a Brotli bug and a Reddit ban.

## Picks

### Cursor Computer Use — Cursor's agents can now click and type in desktop apps and the iOS Simulator
Announced October 23, it lets a Cursor agent build a feature, open the resulting app and test it through the UI, for example saving a note and checking it survives a restart. It's on the radar because it closes the loop between "code compiles" and "app works" beyond the browser, in native and mobile apps. It needs Accessibility and Screen Recording permissions on macOS, so decide deliberately what you let it drive. Details here come from a third-party write-up, so check Cursor's own docs for availability on your plan.

### IQuest-Q1 — a 320B sparse MoE (15B active) built for command-line agent work
The paper, dated October 23, describes a text-only agentic model with 320B total and 15B active parameters and context extended to 512K tokens, aimed at CLI tasks. It's interesting because the authors say weights are released and that the model helped develop its own successors under human supervision. The benchmark scores (SWE-Bench Pro, DeepSWE, CyberGym) are the authors' own, and the excerpt we saw doesn't name a license, so check the model card before building on it.

### Node2Shell (CVE-2026-111497) — a Brotli decoder bug that reaches Node.js, Bun and Envoy
An out-of-bounds write in Brotli's decoder, reportedly found by OpenAI researchers, can be triggered by crafted compressed input without authentication. Node.js shipped fixes on October 21 in 22.23.4, 24.22.1 and 26.12.1; Bun before 1.4.4 and Envoy are affected, and apps that bundle their own Brotli copy need to adopt the fix themselves. Proof-of-concept code exists, though there was no evidence of widespread exploitation as of October 23. Update, and watch anything that decompresses untrusted Brotli, such as `fetch()` or Express 5 body parsing.

### r/opensource bans AI-built projects — a 98k-weekly-visitor community draws a hard line
On October 24 the moderators posted rules that exclude projects that use, are made by, or target AI (MCP servers are named), and require projects to be a year old with multiple contributors. Roughly 1,400 comments followed, with critics pointing out that a literal reading would catch the Linux kernel, and a splinter community, r/TrueOpenSource, appeared. It matters as a signal of how open-source gatekeeping is reacting to a flood of AI-generated project posts. One moderator's policy on one subreddit is not an industry standard.
