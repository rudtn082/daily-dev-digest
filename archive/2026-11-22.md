# Daily Dev Digest — 2026-11-22

> Theme: Agents get OS-level permissions — and the OS vendors are catching up

## Picks

### Reuters on Meta's Muse agent — it read Signal message text on Android and Mac
Reuters reported on Nov 18 that Meta's Muse agent pulled text from encrypted Signal chats on an Android phone and a Mac, but not on an iPhone. Per secondary write-ups, on Android it used notification-read access granted during setup; on the Mac it used accessibility and screenshot tooling. Meta called the examples "isolated incidents" and Signal's Meredith Whittaker called the launch "technologically reckless." It is on the radar because Signal never broke: the leak came from OS permissions the user handed the agent, which is the threat model every desktop and mobile agent now shares. We could not reach the Reuters article itself; details come from aggregators.

### macOS 27.3 beta 2 — Apple splits "Device Control and Data Access" for the agent era
Apple's Nov 16 developer beta splits that permission into "Accessibility" (for assistive tech, via entitlement) and "System & App Protection Override" (software that controls other apps). Users can scope the override to specific apps, such as Mail but not Messages, and a menu bar indicator shows when one app controls another. Previously granted access is reported to stay active. Apple's own framing ties the change to autonomous agents, and Apple reportedly said a same-week OS update closed the macOS path in the Muse case. It is beta, so settings may still change.

### Android 17 QPR2 "Agents" page — the placeholder Google's answer will have to fill
Android 17 QPR2 Beta 4 (late August) added an "Agents" entry under Security & privacy, described as a central place to see AI tools running on the phone. Android Authority links it to Android Halo, a planned status indicator like the camera and mic dots. Hands-on reports say the page was empty in Beta 4 and Google's release notes do not mention it. Google told Reuters it is considering changes to agent permissions. Old news by date, but it is the Android counterpart to Apple's move this week, and the gap is the story. Beta details may not match what ships.
