# Daily Dev Digest — 2026-10-25

> Theme: Models and benchmarks get their receipts — a trillion-scale open-weights bet, an eval for forgotten languages, and AI-found bugs landing in PHP.

## Picks

### Kimi K3.1 Pro — Moonshot's 2.8T-parameter follow-up, with weights promised for early November
Moonshot unveiled K3.1 Pro on October 24: same 2.8T total / 104B active architecture as K3, 1M-token context, text and image input, available now in the Kimi apps and API. It's on the radar because K3 was the largest open-weights model shipped, and K3.1 Pro claims a big jump on agentic evals (Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index 55 vs. 44 for K3). Weights are not out yet, the license wasn't stated, and most numbers are Moonshot's own, so treat the "strongest open model" framing as conditional on the release actually happening.

### MultiLangBench — OpenAI's eval: build a SQLite-compatible engine in 20 languages
Published October 19, the benchmark asks a model to write a SQLite-compatible database engine from scratch in each of 20 languages, including COBOL, Fortran, OCaml and Visual Basic. OpenAI put the evaluation setup and test suite on GitHub, which makes it a rare vendor eval you can actually rerun on other models. The headline scores (GPT-6 Astra averaging 87.4%) are self-reported, and the separate COBOL migration test behind the 86.6% figure is hidden.

### PHP2Shell — AI-assisted audit finds severe PHP flaws, patched October 22
Remote-code-execution bugs in PHP, found with help from GPT-5.6 Sol in an audit run by Calif and tied to OpenAI's Patch the Planet effort with Trail of Bits, shipped in a patch release on October 22 after a partial leak forced the schedule forward. It matters because PHP sits under a huge share of the web and this is a concrete case of AI-found, human-coordinated disclosure. Coverage disagrees on who found what and when, so read the PHP advisory itself and update your PHP version.
